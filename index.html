Privacy Policy — Critical Crossing

Last updated: 26 August 2026

Critical Crossing is developed by SWMI. This policy explains what the game collects, why, and what you can do about it.

The game has no advertising, no in-app purchases, and no account sign-up. You are never asked for your name, email address, phone number or payment details.

What the game collects

1. An anonymous player ID

When you first open the game it signs in anonymously through Firebase Authentication. This produces a random identifier that is not linked to any email address, phone number, Google account or social profile. Its only purposes are to keep one leaderboard row per player and to stop other players overwriting your scores.

The identifier is stored on your device and on our Firebase servers. It is not displayed to other players.

2. Leaderboard entries

If you play Endless or Hardcore mode, the game submits:

FieldWhat it is
Display nameA nickname you choose. Leave it unset and you appear as “Anonymous”.
ScoreThe run’s score
Vehicles clearedHow much traffic you delivered
TimestampWhen the run finished
Anonymous IDAs described above

Please do not use your real name, or anyone else’s, as your display name — it is shown publicly to every other player.

3. Usage and device information (Firebase Analytics)

The game includes Google Firebase Analytics, which reports:

  • when the app is opened, and how long sessions last
  • your device model, operating system version and language
  • an approximate region derived from your IP address — country or city level, never a precise location; the game requests no location permission
  • basic stability information such as unexpected exits

This is used to understand whether players return to the game and where they stop playing. It is not used to identify you and is not sold or shared with advertisers.

The game does not collect the Android Advertising ID. The permission it would need has been deliberately removed from the app, and advertising-ID collection is switched off.

4. Settings stored on your device

Progress, unlocked levels, sound preferences and your chosen nickname are saved locally on your device. This information does not leave the device, except for the leaderboard fields listed above, and is removed when you uninstall the game.

Who processes this data

Google LLC, through Firebase Authentication, Firebase Realtime Database and Firebase Analytics. Their handling is governed by the Firebase Privacy and Security policy and the Google Privacy Policy. Data may be processed on Google servers outside your country.

We do not sell your data, and we share it with no one else.

How long it is kept

Leaderboard entries and run history are kept while the game is operating, so that records remain comparable over time. Analytics data is retained according to Firebase’s default retention period and is then deleted or aggregated by Google.

Deleting your data

Email critical.crossing.game@gmail.com with the display name shown on your leaderboard entry.

What is deleted: your leaderboard row and your full run history — display name, scores, vehicles cleared and timestamps — together with the anonymous identifier they are stored against. This is permanent and cannot be undone.

What is not deleted: usage and device information collected by Firebase Analytics. It is not stored against your leaderboard identity and cannot be traced back to it, so there is nothing there for us to find and remove. It expires on Firebase’s own retention schedule.

Because sign-in is anonymous, your display name is the only way we can locate your records — we have no other means of identifying you.

Uninstalling the game removes everything stored on your device, but does not remove leaderboard entries already submitted.

Children

Critical Crossing is not directed at children under 13. We do not knowingly collect personal information from children. If you believe a child has submitted a display name that identifies them, contact us at the address above and we will remove it.

Changes to this policy

If this policy changes, the date at the top will change with it. Material changes will be noted in the app’s store listing.
